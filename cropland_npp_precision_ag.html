01

What it measures

As crops grow they capture carbon, and satellites estimate how much each year. That yearly figure is net primary productivity (NPP), a direct proxy for how much a growing season produced. Higher NPP on farmland means a more productive year.

This workflow isolates NPP over cropland only, masking out forest, settlement, and water with a satellite land-cover map then asks two questions of the 2001–2024 record: is productivity trending up or down across the region, and where on the map is it changing fastest?

02

How the workflow runs

A fixed pipeline, each stage feeds the next, so the order carries meaning.

01

Define the region

An area of interest is drawn on an interactive map, everything downstream is clipped to it.

02

Locate the cropland

A MODIS land-cover product flags the cropland class, building a yearly cropland mask.

03

Read productivity, masked to fields

Annual NPP is linked to the mask, keeping only cropland pixels and scaling them to real units.

04

Test the regional trend

Yearly cropland totals are tested for a real increasing or decreasing trend, plus a break-point year.

05

Map the change spatially

The same trend test runs on every pixel's time series, mapping where productivity rose or fell.
